    ⧗ Chronoscope

    You are one person, a traveller loose in time, in a world whose history is completely deterministic. Left alone, it plays out the same way every time: peoples settle, farm, multiply, fight, trade ideas, catch plagues, rise into empires and shatter into successor states.

    You can change that, one deed at a time. Walk the land, learn what the people know, carry it to others, dig canals, rally a village, nurse the sick, or slip into a capital and kill a king. Each deed is small. History decides how far it spreads.

    Playing as a person

    • Walk: click the map, or use WASD / arrow keys. Walking a province takes about two months; crossing sea by boat takes half a year.
    • Deeds (keys 1–8) act where you stand and take a year. Greyed-out deeds tell you why they won't work here.
    • Rest (R) lets a year pass. Press Play to let time flow on its own.
    • Travel in time by dragging the history chart. Acting later in your own timeline continues it; going back and acting starts a new one, so you can compare.

    Seeing your ripples

    • Lives changed counts the people who live, or don't, compared with the timeline you came from. It moves at once. The share of the world ruled differently often takes centuries.
    • Chronicle: your deeds appear in gold. After a change, new events are history you created and erased ones you prevented. Events after the present year are foretold, in italics.
    • The Powers on the left let you act as a god instead: plague, floods, new mountains.

    The rules of the world

    • Land: soil feeds people; farming depletes it, fallow land recovers. Rivers and coasts are rich.
    • People: grow toward what the land can feed, starve in droughts, spill over into empty land.
    • Solidarity (Ibn Khaldun's asabiya): grows on contested frontiers, decays in comfortable interiors.
    • War: power = solidarity × population × technology, fading with distance from the capital.
    • Ideas: technology grows with population, crosses borders, and is partly lost when realms fall.
    • Collapse: an empire whose solidarity stays low too long shatters into successor states.
    • Plague and climate: crowded cities breed epidemics; regional droughts and golden ages come and go.
